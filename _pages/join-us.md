# Join Us

The Adaptive Catalysis Group welcomes motivated researchers and students who are interested in understanding and controlling **dynamic catalyst evolution under reaction conditions**.

Our work sits at the interface of catalyst synthesis, porous materials, synchrotron science, and reaction-mechanism studies. We are particularly interested in researchers who want to connect careful materials design with rigorous structural evidence.

---

## Research Areas

Potential projects may involve:

- dynamic restructuring and deactivation of heterogeneous catalysts
- atomically precise single-site and multinuclear metal catalysts
- zeolites and other porous catalytic materials
- operando X-ray and neutron diffraction
- total scattering and pair distribution function analysis
- X-ray absorption spectroscopy
- structure–function relationships in catalytic reactions

---

## Prospective PhD Students

Students with backgrounds in chemistry, chemical engineering, materials science, catalysis, or related areas are welcome to make contact.

Strong applicants should be interested in developing the ability to:

- define mechanistic scientific questions rather than only execute experiments
- design discriminating control experiments
- evaluate competing structural or mechanistic hypotheses
- integrate synthesis, catalytic testing, and advanced characterization
- communicate scientific results clearly and critically

## Postdoctoral Researchers and Research Assistants

Researchers with experience in heterogeneous catalysis, porous materials, synchrotron characterization, crystallography, XAS, PDF analysis, or related areas are encouraged to contact us when suitable opportunities arise.

This page does not imply that a funded vacancy is currently open. Specific funded positions will be announced through the group website or relevant institutional channels.

## Collaborations

We welcome collaborations that benefit from complementary expertise in catalyst synthesis, operando experiments, synchrotron characterization, structural modelling, and catalytic reaction analysis.
